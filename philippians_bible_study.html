Philippians 1

Joy in hardship

Chapter overview

Paul thanks the Philippians for their partnership in the gospel. He explains how his imprisonment has helped advance the message of Christ and encourages believers to live in a manner worthy of the gospel, standing united in faith.

Key themes

  • Gratitude and partnership in ministry
  • Confidence that God continues His work in believers
  • Seeing hardship through the perspective of the gospel
  • Courage, unity, and a life worthy of Christ

Read and observe

Read: Philippians 1:3–11, 12–18, 20–30 (NIV)

Look for: What brings Paul joy? How does he interpret his circumstances? What kind of unity does he ask the church to demonstrate?

Put it into practice

Where can you choose gratitude or courage in a difficult situation this week?

Discussion questions

  1. What does Paul's prayer in verses 9–11 reveal about the kind of growth he desires for the church?
  2. How did Paul's imprisonment contribute to the spread of the gospel?
  3. What does it mean to conduct yourself in a manner worthy of the gospel?
  4. How can a church remain united when its members face opposition or disagreement?
Philippians 2

Humble service

Chapter overview

Paul calls the church to unity, humility, and concern for others. He points to Jesus' humility and obedience, then highlights the faithful service of Timothy and Epaphroditus as examples for the community.

Key themes

  • Unity through humility and unselfish love
  • Jesus' willing humility and obedience
  • Living faithfully as God's people
  • Serving others with sincere concern

Read and observe

Read: Philippians 2:1–11, 12–18, 19–30 (NIV)

Look for: What attitude does Paul encourage? What does Jesus' example teach about greatness? What qualities distinguish Timothy and Epaphroditus?

Put it into practice

Identify one practical way to put another person's needs ahead of your own this week.

Discussion questions

  1. Which attitudes in verses 1–4 help protect unity in a church or family?
  2. What do Jesus' actions in verses 5–11 teach us about humility and obedience?
  3. How should believers understand Paul's instruction to work out their salvation?
  4. What can we learn from Timothy's and Epaphroditus' examples of service?
Philippians 3

Knowing Christ

Chapter overview

Paul warns against confidence in human credentials and describes the surpassing worth of knowing Christ. He does not claim to have reached perfection; instead, he presses on toward the goal and reminds believers that their citizenship is in heaven.

Key themes

  • Confidence in Christ rather than personal achievement
  • The value of knowing Jesus
  • Spiritual growth as an ongoing pursuit
  • Heavenly citizenship and future hope

Read and observe

Read: Philippians 3:1–11, 12–16, 17–21 (NIV)

Look for: What does Paul count as loss? What goal is he pursuing? How does heavenly citizenship shape daily life?

Put it into practice

What achievement, status, or habit might compete with making Christ your highest priority?

Discussion questions

  1. Why does Paul reject confidence in religious or personal credentials?
  2. What does it mean to know Christ and the power of His resurrection?
  3. How does Paul's picture of pressing on encourage believers who feel spiritually unfinished?
  4. In practical terms, how should heavenly citizenship influence our choices?
Philippians 4

Peace and contentment

Chapter overview

Paul urges believers to stand firm, resolve conflict, rejoice, pray, and focus their minds on what is good. He describes learning contentment in both need and plenty and thanks the Philippians for their generous partnership.

Key themes

  • Reconciliation and steadfastness
  • Prayer, thanksgiving, and God's peace
  • Training our thoughts toward what is good
  • Contentment through Christ and generous giving

Read and observe

Read: Philippians 4:1–9, 10–20, 21–23 (NIV)

Look for: What does Paul say to do instead of being anxious? What should shape our thoughts? What has Paul learned about contentment?

Put it into practice

Turn one current worry into a specific prayer of request and thanksgiving.

Discussion questions

  1. What steps does Paul recommend when believers experience anxiety?
  2. Why is it important to practice the thought habits described in verses 8–9?
  3. What does Paul's experience teach about contentment in changing circumstances?
  4. How can a community express generosity and partnership in the gospel?

Closing reflection

Across all four chapters, Paul points us toward a Christ-centered life: joyful in hardship, humble in relationships, focused on knowing Jesus, and peaceful and content through prayer and trust.
